This guide keeps my headphone shortlist alongside the source checks needed before buying. I have not run a controlled comparison of these players, so it does not rank their sound.

## The two players

These are models and approximate price ranges recorded in the October 2026 comparison, retained in this November revision. Check model, region and current offer before buying.

| | iPod Video 5.5G, Rockbox | Sony NW-ZX707 / WM1 series |
|---|---|---|
| Models | Video 5.5G | ZX707 and WM1 variants; specifications differ |
| DAC | Wolfson WM8758 | Modern DAC, Sony's own amp stage |
| Formats | 16-bit/44.1 kHz in practice | Hi-res PCM and DSD |
| Output impedance | Measured at roughly 3–11 Ω, varies with frequency | Not published by Sony; I have not found a bench measurement |
| Outputs | 3.5 mm | 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm balanced |
| Streaming | None | Android, Wi-Fi, streaming apps |
| Price | Second-hand plus a storage mod | About $900 to $3,700 |

The [format guide](/technical-notes/what-you-can-actually-hear/) explains sample rate and bit depth. Format support alone does not establish an audible ranking.

For IEM pairing, verify output impedance rather than assuming it from the model or price. The practical comparison below separates features from unmeasured sound differences.

## Comparison priorities

| Priority | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| Offline music | File support, playlist handling and usable storage |
| Streaming | Required apps and network support |
| Headphones | Output noise, available level and impedance for the actual pairing |
| Battery | Measured runtime under comparable playback conditions |
| Controls | Click wheel or touchscreen, and ease of use |
| Cost | Player, storage, repairs and accessories together |

I have not run a controlled listening comparison of these players. Numerical sound scores would imply evidence I do not have.

## Reasons to consider the Walkman

Streaming apps, wireless connections and its available outputs may be useful. Check the regional model's specifications and the headphones you intend to use. I do not assume an output is quieter or lower impedance simply because the player costs more.

## Why I keep the iPod

I like its controls and offline playback. My [setup guide](/technical-notes/ipod-video-5-5g-rockbox-purist-player/) records the hardware and pending 256 GB upgrade. Battery runtime remains under test; I am not ranking it against a manufacturer's quoted figure.

## Check the actual pairing

The [output impedance guide](/technical-notes/dac-dongles-daps-and-output-impedance/) explains how a source can change the response of a varying load. I do not have measurements for every combination below. Driver count and price are not substitutes for those measurements.

Compare the IEM's impedance curve, source noise and available level. Where a figure is missing, compatibility remains an open question.

## My buying order

1. **Fit first:** try the supplied tips before replacing equipment.
2. **Crinear Daybreak:** a candidate from my neutral shortlist; check fit and source compatibility.
3. **Softears Volume S:** a candidate when comparing bass-oriented tunings.
4. **Thieaudio Origin:** a more expensive alternative to audition, not an automatic quality upgrade.
5. **Another player:** consider one when you need a specific connection, app or measured output capability.

This is a shopping order, not a listening-test ranking.

## Settings on both players

For a comparison, use consistent processing settings. My everyday Rockbox settings include crossfeed by preference.

| Setting | iPod, Rockbox | Sony Walkman |
|---|---|---|
| Equaliser | Off | Off |
| Tone controls | Bass and treble at 0 | Off |
| Enhancement | Meier crossfeed on, as in Part 7 | DSEE and ClearAudio+ off |
| Bypass mode | Not applicable | Direct Source on, which skips the processing |
| Dithering | On, for sources above 16-bit | Not applicable |
| Volume | Keep digital volume below clipping | Keep it in the normal range; use high gain only if needed |

Crossfeed is a reasonable exception if old hard-panned stereo recordings tire you; it is a comfort feature, not a fix. The same goes for a small EQ bass shelf on a neutral IEM when you want weight for one album. The point of turning everything off is to know what the neutral starting point sounds like, not to forbid changes.

## What a comparison can establish

I have not run a controlled blind comparison of these players. Use the same recording and headphones, match levels and keep processing settings consistent. A product's price or driver layout cannot substitute for a measurement of its output or impedance curve.
