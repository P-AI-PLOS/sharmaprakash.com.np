IEM listings lead with driver counts: "1DD + 4BA + 2EST". That line tells you how the earphone is built. It does not tell you how it sounds. The tuning does that, and the tuning is what [the part on sound signatures](/technical-notes/iem-sound-signatures-explained/) was about.

This post covers what the driver types are, what the impedance and sensitivity numbers on the box mean, and why some IEMs sound different on different players. It ends with picks for each signature.

## The driver types

| Driver | Construction |
| --- | --- |
| Dynamic | A voice coil moves a diaphragm. |
| Balanced armature | A small armature drives a diaphragm through a mechanical link. |
| Planar magnetic | A conductor on a diaphragm moves in a magnetic field. |
| Electrostatic | An electric field moves a diaphragm; implementation varies. |
| Piezoelectric | A material changes shape under an applied voltage. |

Hybrids combine driver types. Crossovers divide the frequency range between them. These choices describe construction; they do not guarantee bass quality, detail or a particular sound signature.

## Driver count is not a quality score

A driver count describes construction. It does not establish tuning, distortion, comfort or value. Compare [frequency-response measurements](/technical-notes/reading-iem-frequency-response-graphs/) and listen with a consistent [fit](/technical-notes/iem-fit-eartips-and-cables/).

## Impedance and sensitivity

Impedance describes the electrical load; sensitivity describes the level produced for a stated input. Check whether sensitivity uses dB/mW or dB/V before comparing figures.

An IEM's impedance can change with frequency. The source's output impedance then affects the voltage reaching it at different frequencies. Driver count alone cannot tell you whether a pairing changes the sound. The [source guide](/technical-notes/dac-dongles-daps-and-output-impedance/) explains the checks.

I left out premium picks for bright, V-shape and vocal tunings. I could not confirm current prices and driver layouts for the sets I had in mind, and a guessed spec is worse than no spec.

### My buying shortlist

- **Purist who sometimes wants bass:** start with the Daybreak. Use a gentle low-shelf on the player when you want weight. If you find yourself using that shelf every day, the Volume S or Origin is the next step.
- **Older player with some output impedance:** stay with the Gate, Hexa or Daybreak. These are candidates, not verified pairings; check impedance measurements before buying.
- **Modern low-impedance player:** the RSV and Origin remain candidates, subject to fit, tuning and measured source compatibility.
- **Not sure what you like:** buy the Gate first. At about $22 it is a cheap way to learn whether neutral suits you.
