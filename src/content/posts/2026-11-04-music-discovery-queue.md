A recommendation is easy to save and easy to forget. After enough saves, a discovery list can become a warehouse of good intentions rather than a source of music you actually play.

Use two places: an inbox for possibilities and a small active queue for the next few listening sessions. The distinction makes adding a suggestion different from committing attention to it.

## Keep the reason for the recommendation

When you save an album, add a short reason: recommended by a friend, related to an artist you enjoy, or interesting because of a particular instrument or mood. That reason helps you choose later.

A bare title can lose its context quickly. You may not remember whether it was a strong recommendation or something you saved while distracted.

## Choose for a real session

Move an album into the active queue when you can imagine when you will hear it. A quiet evening, a walk, and background work call for different kinds of listening.

Do not fill the active queue with more music than you reasonably expect to explore soon. The exact number is personal. The point is to make selection easy when the session begins.

## Mix familiarity and uncertainty

An active queue can include one adjacent recommendation and one less familiar choice. This keeps discovery from becoming either a stream of near-duplicates or an exhausting demand for novelty.

Treat automated recommendations as candidates. You can use the reason a system provides as a clue without accepting its explanation as a fact about your taste.

## Close the loop lightly

After listening, record whether you want to return, explore further, or move on. A sentence is enough. If you did not finish because the context was wrong, distinguish that from disliking the album.

Move completed items out of the active queue. Keep a separate favorites or revisit list so the queue remains a place for decisions rather than a permanent archive.

## Let old suggestions expire

An inbox item does not become more important because it has been waiting longer. Periodically remove suggestions whose reason no longer interests you. You can always rediscover them.
